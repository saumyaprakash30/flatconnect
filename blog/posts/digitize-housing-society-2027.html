How to Digitize Your Housing Society in 2027: A Step-by-Step Guide

Digital Transformation, Society Management November 10, 2026 By Saumya Prakash 6 min read

Promised yourself your society would finally get off the paper register "next year"? Next year is 2027, and the start of a year is a good time to switch. Bills for a new cycle are going out anyway, committees are often new, and residents expect some changes in January.

This guide covers the whole switch, from the data you'll need to the day you put the old diary away. It's written for small and mid-sized societies and RWAs, with no IT person and no budget for consultants.

Why Start the Switch Before January

If you wait until the 2027 bills are due, you'll be setting up software and chasing payments in the same month. It's easier to do the groundwork in November and December:

  • Clean closing figures. Your dues and balances at the end of the year become the opening figures in the app. A natural cut-off date makes them easier to agree on.
  • Time for a trial run. The committee can use the app for a few weeks before residents ever see it.
  • One announcement. "From January, bills come through the app" is simpler for residents than a change halfway through a cycle.

Step 1: Gather What You Already Have

Before you open any app, put these in one place, ideally one spreadsheet:

  1. Every tower, flat and shop the society bills, including commercial properties if you have them.
  2. Who lives where: each owner's name and phone number, plus tenants where they apply.
  3. What each flat owes today: pending dues, plus any advance a resident has already paid.
  4. Your bank and cash balances on the cut-off date.
  5. Key documents: bylaws, the last AGM minutes, the building insurance policy and any service contracts.

Agree the pending-dues figures with the treasurer before you import them. A wrong opening balance causes more arguments than anything else.

Step 2: Add Your Flats

In FlatConnect you can type flats in one by one or import them, along with their past dues, from a spreadsheet. Small societies can type them in over a cup of tea. With more than a few dozen flats, the import saves a lot of time. A checklist on the manager dashboard shows what's done and what's left.

Step 3: Set the Maintenance Plan

Choose the amount and billing cycle (monthly or quarterly) for each flat or property. If your society charges by flat size or charges shops differently, set that up here rather than adjusting bills by hand later. If you're also revising the amount for 2027, our guide to setting maintenance for 2027 covers the decisions to make first.

Step 4: Set Up Your Accounts

Add the society's bank and cash accounts with their opening balances. From then on, collections and expenses are recorded against these accounts, so the balance in the app should always match your bank statement. Do a month-end reconciliation to keep them in step.

Step 5: Try It With the Committee First

Spend a week or two using the app inside the committee before anyone else is invited:

  • Send a notice to yourselves, with an attachment.
  • Record an expense, such as the security agency bill.
  • Make one real online payment against a committee member's flat and check the receipt.
  • Record one cash payment and confirm it as the treasurer would.
  • Upload the bylaws to the Society Library and decide what residents can see and what stays committee-only.

Anything confusing at this stage is far easier to fix with five people than with fifty.

Step 6: Invite Residents

Owners and tenants sign in with their phone number, so nobody needs a password. The app is available in English and Hindi, on Android and in any web browser. iPhone users can use the web app at app.flatconnect.in.

Some things that help with the rollout:

  • Explain why first. Instant receipts, dues you can check any time, and accounts anyone can see are reasons residents care about.
  • Start with the willing. Residents who are comfortable with apps can then help their neighbours.
  • Make a short "how to pay" note with two or three screenshots and share it in the society WhatsApp group.
  • Visit older residents in person. Sitting with them for one payment usually does it.

Step 7: Send the First Bills

Once flats, plans and residents are in, send the first bills. Residents see their dues right away and can pay online in the app. People who pay by UPI to the society's QR or by bank transfer can report the payment from the app for the treasurer to confirm. The society's payment QR can be printed on bills and invoices too.

Due and overdue reminders go out on WhatsApp, and residents can report a UPI or bank payment straight from the reminder.

Step 8: Move the Rest Over

After the first cycle, bring over the other things the society still does on paper:

  • The gate: replace the paper visitor register with entry and exit logs, visitor photos for residents to approve, and pre-approved guests and deliveries. See our visitor management guide.
  • Domestic help: keep a registry of maids, cooks and drivers, verified by the manager and linked to the flats they work for.
  • Vehicles: record vehicles with their RFID stickers and parking slots.
  • Documents: add renewal reminders for insurance and contracts so nothing lapses without anyone noticing.

What Usually Goes Wrong

Importing dues nobody agreed on

If a resident disputes their opening balance, everything after it gets questioned too. Settle the figures first, and record any later corrections in the app so the history is kept.

Running two systems for too long

Keeping the old register as a backup for one cycle is sensible. Keeping it for six months means nobody trusts either one. Choose a date and stop using the register.

One person doing everything

If only the treasurer knows how the app works, you're back to one point of failure. Give each committee member a role with the access their job needs.

Your 2027 Checklist

  1. Opening dues agreed and imported
  2. Maintenance plan set for every flat and property
  3. Bank and cash accounts added with opening balances
  4. Committee trial done, including one real payment
  5. Residents invited, with a "how to pay" note shared
  6. First 2027 bills sent from the app
  7. Bylaws and AGM minutes in the Society Library
  8. A date set to stop using the paper register

The Bottom Line

Going digital in 2027 mostly comes down to getting your opening figures right, trying the app out inside the committee, and bringing residents in gradually. Do the groundwork before January and your society starts the year with the 2027 bills already coming from the app.
