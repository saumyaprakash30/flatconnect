AGM and Audit Prep for 2027: A Checklist for Society Treasurers and Secretaries

Finance & Accounts November 10, 2026 By Saumya Prakash 4 min read

The AGM shouldn't be the one week a year the treasurer barely sleeps. In many societies it still is: receipts piled on the dining table, bank statements printed and highlighted, and a balance sheet put together the night before, which someone then questions in the first five minutes.

This checklist is for treasurers and secretaries who want the 2027 AGM and audit to be routine. Most of the work happens during the year, a little each month, so that by the time the meeting comes round there's not much left to do.

Check your own rules first

When the AGM must be held, how much notice members need, the quorum, and whether your accounts need an external auditor all depend on your bylaws and on the rules for your type of society in your state. Use this checklist alongside them, not instead of them.

All Year: Habits That Make the AGM Easy

Record payments as they happen

Every collection should be recorded against a flat and an account when it comes in, not batched up at the end of the quarter. In FlatConnect, online payments are recorded with a receipt automatically. Cash and bank payments that residents report wait for the treasurer to confirm, so nothing gets counted twice or missed.

Log expenses with the bill

Record each expense, whether the security agency, electricity, lift AMC or housekeeping, against the bank or cash account it was paid from. A record made the day the bill is paid needs no detective work at audit time.

Reconcile every month

Compare each account's balance in the app with the bank statement at month-end. Finding a ₹500 difference in March is a five-minute job. Finding twelve months of small differences in one go can take a whole weekend.

Correct mistakes properly

When a payment goes against the wrong flat, correct it with a proper entry rather than deleting it. FlatConnect keeps the full history of corrections, which is what an auditor wants to see.

Six Weeks Before: The Treasurer's Checklist

  1. Close the last month. Make sure every account is reconciled up to the end of the period you're reporting on.
  2. Look at the defaulters. List flats with long-pending dues and decide what the committee will propose at the meeting, so it isn't argued over on the night.
  3. Check advances. Residents who have paid ahead should see that reflected. Advances are adjusted against future dues, so check they haven't been counted as income for the period.
  4. Review late fees. Check that late fees applied this year followed the rule the society agreed, and note any waivers the committee approved.
  5. Generate the reports. Get the balance sheet, ledger and PDF reports from the app for the period.
  6. Send them to the auditor if your society uses one, along with access to the supporting records.

Four Weeks Before: The Secretary's Checklist

  1. Confirm the date, venue and agenda with the committee.
  2. Send the AGM notice to every resident as a notice in the app, with the agenda and draft accounts attached. New notices stay highlighted for residents until they've read them.
  3. Check the member list. Make sure owners and tenants are recorded correctly, since it may matter who can vote under your bylaws.
  4. Collect last year's minutes from the Society Library so action items can be followed up.
  5. Check document renewals. Insurance, lift certificates and service contracts that expire soon may need a decision at the meeting.

On the Day

  • Bring the balance sheet and ledger, and have the app open in case someone asks about a particular payment or expense.
  • When a resident asks "where did this ₹40,000 go?", show the entry and the account it was paid from. Being able to answer on the spot does more for trust than any slide.
  • Note resolutions, budget approvals and any change to maintenance for the year ahead. That change then goes into the maintenance plan.

After the AGM

  1. Upload the minutes to the Society Library and decide whether they're visible to everyone or to the committee only.
  2. Send a short broadcast summarising the decisions, for the residents who couldn't attend.
  3. Update the maintenance plan if the meeting approved a new amount or billing cycle.
  4. If a new committee was elected, work through a proper handover. Our committee handover guide covers it.

The Bottom Line

A calm AGM comes from the eleven months before it. Record payments and expenses when they happen, reconcile each month, correct mistakes with a proper entry, and the 2027 accounts will be nearly ready on their own. See how FlatConnect's accounting and billing fits into this.
