Setting Your Society's Maintenance for 2027: Amounts, Billing Cycles, Late Fees and Reminders

Finance & Payments November 10, 2026 By Saumya Prakash 4 min read

Every year someone at the society meeting asks: "Why are we paying this much, and why can't it stay the same?" A clear answer, backed by numbers, is the difference between a quick approval and a two-hour argument.

This guide goes through the decisions a committee makes when setting maintenance for 2027: the amount, the billing cycle, late fees, advances and reminders. It also shows how each one is set up in FlatConnect.

Step 1: Work Out What 2027 Will Cost

Start from what you actually spent, not from last year's maintenance amount. Pull this year's expenses by category:

  • Staff and services: security, housekeeping, gardening
  • Utilities: common-area electricity, water, diesel for the generator
  • Maintenance contracts: lift, pumps, fire equipment, generator
  • Insurance and statutory costs
  • Repairs: the one-offs that "only happen sometimes" but somehow happen every year

Then adjust for what you already know about 2027: a new security contract, a lift AMC renewal, a rate change the agency has announced. If you've recorded expenses against categories all year, this is a report you print rather than a weekend spent with a calculator.

Don't forget a reserve

Painting, waterproofing, pump replacements and lift overhauls will come. Many societies set aside part of each month's maintenance for them so they don't have to raise a sudden special charge. How much is up to your committee and your bylaws, but agree an amount and present it openly.

Step 2: Decide How to Split It

Common approaches in Indian societies are:

ApproachWorks well whenWatch out for
Flat rate per flatFlats are similar in sizeOwners of smaller flats feeling they pay too much
By area (per sq. ft.)Flat sizes vary a lotNeeding accurate area records for every flat
Different rate for commercial unitsShops or offices share the buildingAgreeing the rate with commercial owners

Whatever you choose, follow your bylaws and record the decision in the meeting minutes. In FlatConnect, the maintenance plan sets the amount for each flat or property, so different rates for different units don't need manual adjustments every cycle.

Step 3: Monthly or Quarterly?

  • Monthly amounts are smaller and easier for residents to fit into their budget, and the society's cash flow is steadier. There are more bills and more reminders.
  • Quarterly means fewer bills and less follow-up, but the amounts are bigger, and one missed payment leaves a bigger gap.

FlatConnect supports both. Choose what suits your residents, and keep it the same all year so people know when to expect a bill.

Step 4: Agree a Late Fee Rule, and Stick to It

Late fees work when they're predictable and the same for everyone. They cause resentment when they're applied to some flats and quietly waived for others.

  • Agree the rule at the meeting: a fixed amount or interest, and after how many days.
  • Check your bylaws and any state rules on limits for interest or penalties.
  • Set it once in the app so it's applied the same way to every overdue account, with no manual tracking and no awkward conversations.
  • If the committee waives a fee, record why.

Step 5: Plan for Residents Who Pay in Advance

Some residents prefer to pay six months or a year at once. That's good for cash flow, as long as it's recorded properly. In FlatConnect, an advance is adjusted against future dues automatically, so the resident never gets a bill for something they've already paid and the treasurer doesn't have to track it in a separate spreadsheet.

Step 6: Make Paying Easy

The fewer steps between a bill and a payment, the more residents pay on time:

  • Residents see their dues in the app and can pay online, with an instant receipt.
  • Those who prefer UPI to the society's QR or a bank transfer can report the payment from the app for the treasurer to confirm.
  • The society's payment QR can be printed on every bill and invoice.

Step 7: Let Reminders Do the Chasing

No treasurer enjoys messaging neighbours about money. FlatConnect sends due and overdue reminders on WhatsApp, and residents can report a UPI or bank payment straight from the reminder. The treasurer can see who has paid without asking anyone. More on this in our payment reminder guide.

Presenting the 2027 Amount to Residents

  1. Show this year's actual expenses by category.
  2. Show the known increases for 2027, and the reason for each.
  3. Show the reserve contribution separately, so it isn't hidden in the total.
  4. Give the proposed amount per flat, or per sq. ft., and the billing cycle.
  5. After approval, send the decision as a notice so every resident has it in writing.

The Bottom Line

A maintenance amount residents accept comes from real expense figures, a fair way of splitting them, and rules applied the same way to everyone.
