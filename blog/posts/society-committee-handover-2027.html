Committee Handover in 2027: How to Pass On Society Records Without Losing Anything

Society Management November 10, 2026 By Saumya Prakash 4 min read

Some society records last one committee term. The outgoing treasurer hands over a carry bag of files, a spreadsheet only they understand, and a phone number "in case you have questions". Six months later nobody can say why flat B-204 shows ₹12,000 pending.

If your society is electing a new committee in 2027, a planned handover avoids that. This guide covers what to hand over, in what order, and how to make sure the next handover is easier.

Why Handovers Go Wrong

  • The records live with a person. A diary, a laptop, a WhatsApp chat: when that person steps down, the records go with them.
  • The balances don't match. The new committee starts from figures they can't check, and every later dispute traces back to them.
  • Access is never changed. Former members still have logins, bank mandates or guard PINs long after they've left.

Before the Election: What the Outgoing Committee Should Do

  1. Reconcile all accounts up to a clear cut-off date, with the bank and cash balances in the records matching the statements and the cash box.
  2. Settle pending confirmations. Confirm or reject any cash and bank payments residents have reported but nobody has checked yet.
  3. Note the open items: unpaid vendor bills, disputed dues, ongoing repairs, and quotes waiting for a decision.
  4. Get the documents in order: bylaws, AGM minutes, insurance, contracts and registration papers. In FlatConnect, these live in the Society Library rather than in someone's cupboard.

When the records are already in a shared app

FlatConnect stores your society's records in the cloud, not on one person's phone or laptop. Bills, payments, accounts, residents and documents stay where they are when the committee changes, so the handover is mostly about changing who has access.

Handover Day: The Checklist

1. Money

  • Agree the closing balance of every bank and cash account, and have both committees sign off on it.
  • Go through the dues report together: who owes what, and since when.
  • List advances residents have paid that will be adjusted against future dues.
  • Bring the balance sheet and ledger up to the cut-off date.
  • Update bank signatories with the bank. This happens outside any app and is easy to forget.

2. People and access

  • Roles and permissions: give each incoming member the access their role needs, so the treasurer gets the accounts and the secretary gets notices and documents. Then change the outgoing members' access.
  • App lock: new managers should turn on the extra app lock for manager accounts, on top of their login.
  • The gate: check which guards have access to which gates, and change codes and PINs if guards or the security agency are changing as well.

3. Residents and property

  • Check the owner and tenant records against who actually lives where. When tenants move out, mark the move-out instead of deleting them, so the history is kept.
  • Check the vehicle list, RFID stickers and parking slot assignments.
  • Check the domestic-help registry, so every maid, cook and driver is still verified and linked to the right flats.

4. Documents and deadlines

  • Check that every important document is in the Society Library with the right visibility: everyone, or the committee only.
  • Make sure renewal reminders are set for insurance and contracts, so the new committee gets the alert instead of the old one.

The First Month for the New Committee

  1. Read the activity log. Authorised managers can review important admin actions. It's a quick way to see what was done in the last few weeks of the old committee.
  2. Send an introduction broadcast telling residents who's on the new committee, what each person handles and how to reach them.
  3. Run one full billing cycle yourselves before changing anything big, such as the maintenance amount.
  4. Keep the outgoing treasurer on call for a month, for questions only, without manager access.

Make the Next Handover Easier

The best handover is one where there's very little to hand over. If payments, expenses, documents and resident records are all kept in the society's app as things happen, the next election is only a matter of changing roles. That's the kind of record-keeping FlatConnect for RWAs is built for.
